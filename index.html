Longitudinal brain MRI

Make interval change easier to see.

Lävi Delta is a second-reader tool for follow-up brain MRI. It brings candidate changes between baseline and follow-up into one focused, auditable review — and says so explicitly when the comparison cannot be trusted.

01 — The problem

Why longitudinal review is hard

A follow-up brain MRI asks a different question from a first read: not what is there, but what has changed. Answering it means holding two examinations in mind at once — often acquired months apart, on different scanners, with different positioning and protocols.

Interval change is easy to miss precisely because of how it presents. It can be subtle, slow, or scattered across the volume, and the comparison is done pair by pair, largely in the reader's head, under time pressure. The prior may not align with the current study slice for slice; nothing about the display guarantees that the same anatomy is being compared.

The hardest part is usually invisible: knowing whether the two studies can be compared at all. When acquisition or positioning differs too much, a confident side-by-side reading can be confidently wrong. Any tool that supports this task has to take that possibility as seriously as the comparison itself.

02 — The workflow

What Lävi Delta does

  1. Longitudinal pair received

    The baseline and follow-up examinations arrive over standard DICOM from the existing workflow. Processing runs inside the hospital network without operator interaction; no image data needs to leave the institution.

  2. Registration and technical quality assessment

    The follow-up is registered to the baseline, and the comparison itself is assessed before any analysis: is this pair technically fit to compare? The outcome is graded — report, report with caution, or an explicit refusal to report the longitudinal comparison.

  3. Candidate change analysis

    Candidate regions of change are identified with their location, volume and direction, and ranked in two separate lanes: new or increasing, and resolving or decreasing.

  4. Focused radiologist review

    Candidates are presented to the reading radiologist for review. Every candidate is explicitly accepted or rejected by the radiologist; the software makes no diagnostic decision, and the radiologist reads the study independently.

  5. Structured, auditable record

    Results return to the existing workflow as standard DICOM objects — segmentation objects, overlay series and a structured report — with the run's provenance recorded and bound to them.

03 — Clinical domains

Where interval review matters first

  • Stratum I

    Multiple sclerosis

    Regular surveillance MRI, where the appearance of new activity between visits informs treatment decisions.

  • Stratum II

    Primary brain tumours

    Post-treatment follow-up, where slow change must be separated from stable appearance across a long series of examinations.

  • Stratum III

    Brain metastases

    Follow-up after treatment, where small new findings and change in known findings both matter.

Lävi Delta is being developed for adults (18+) undergoing longitudinal brain MRI surveillance. These three settings are the strata of the planned reader study. It is not intended for paediatric imaging, non-brain anatomy, other modalities, or acute stroke triage.

04 — Validation

Clinical validation programme

Whether Lävi Delta actually helps radiologists is a question we will answer with an independent reader study, not with a claim. In the planned study, the same retrospective cases will be read by several radiologists — first without the software, and later with it — so that the study can measure whether the software changes reader performance, while accounting for differences between readers and between cases.

The study will cover one hundred cases across the three clinical settings above, with an independent reference-standard process and independent statistical input. Its final design is subject to methodology review.

05 — Safety philosophy

Quality and abstention

  • Abstention is implemented, not aspirational

    When the prerequisites for a valid longitudinal comparison are not met, Lävi Delta says so explicitly and withholds the longitudinal result — rather than producing a number of unknown reliability.

  • Anatomical correspondence is never inferred from a method name

    Equal geometry, successful registration and convergence are recorded as provenance. They are not treated as proof that the anatomy corresponds. Where correspondence is unsupported, that lane abstains.

  • Provenance travels with the result

    Registration method, policy identity, input hashes and environment are recorded for every run and bound to the exported objects. On the path where the claim can be made, registration is deterministic and replayable: the same input reproduces the same result.

  • A second reader, not a substitute

    The absence of a flagged candidate is not evidence that nothing changed. The radiologist reads every study independently; Lävi Delta is designed to support that reading, not to replace it.

REPORT

The comparison meets its prerequisites and is reported.

REPORT WITH CAUTION

The comparison is reported together with its identified limitations.

COMPARISON WITHHELD

The longitudinal result is explicitly refused, and the refusal is stated.

06 — Technology

Built to be checked

  • Runs inside the hospital

    Lävi Delta is deployed on-premise. It receives studies over standard DICOM, processes them without operator interaction, and returns standard DICOM objects into the existing PACS workflow.

  • Traceability is machine-checked

    The build fails when a requirement cites a test that does not exist, or when a controlled document drifts out of agreement with the code. Traceability is enforced, not merely tabulated.

  • Verified end to end

    The pipeline and the reader environment have been verified to work together end to end against a real deployment — including verifying that rejected cases are rejected.

08 — Development status

What exists, and what is not yet proven

Built and verified

  • The on-premise pipeline: standard DICOM in, automated processing, standard DICOM objects out.
  • Quality-control gating with graded outcomes, including explicit abstention.
  • Per-run provenance, bound to the exported results.
  • A dedicated reader workstation for the planned study — with arm blinding, washout enforcement, role-based access and an audit trail, implemented and tested.
  • Machine-checked traceability from requirements to tests, enforced in the build, with a large continuously run automated test suite.
  • The full producer-to-reader chain, verified against a real deployment.

Not yet proven

  • Clinical performance. Whether Lävi Delta changes reader performance has not been established — that is what the planned reader study exists to measure.
  • Cross-vendor and cross-protocol comparison: a design goal and an explicit requirement of the validation programme, not an established capability.
  • Capabilities still under technical evaluation, which are not part of any claim.
  • Regulatory conformity: Lävi Delta is not CE-marked and is not available for sale.

Lävi Delta is under development. It is not CE-marked, is not available for sale, and must not be used for clinical decision-making. Information on this site describes a product in development and its planned evaluation, not the performance of a marketed medical device.

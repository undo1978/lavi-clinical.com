Technology

Built to be checked.

This page describes the properties of Lävi Delta that survive implementation change. It deliberately does not describe model internals, check counts or thresholds: those change, and a page that names them becomes false without anyone noticing.

Clinical validation and regulatory development in progress · not CE-marked

01 — Deployment

Inside the hospital, on standard DICOM

Lävi Delta runs on-premise, inside the hospital network. It receives studies over standard DICOM, processes them without operator interaction, and returns its results into the existing PACS workflow. No image data needs to leave the institution.

Results are standard DICOM objects: DICOM SEG segmentation objects for candidate regions, secondary-capture overlay series, and a structured report. Nothing about the integration requires a proprietary viewer or a new workflow around the reading radiologist.

The shipped software is an integration and orchestration runtime for a clinical site's own infrastructure — deliberately not a sealed appliance, and not something we present as one-command clinical deployment.

02 — Input handling

Sequence-aware, and fails closed

The pipeline identifies the series it needs from DICOM header evidence, including vendor private tags. When that evidence is insufficient, it fails closed with an explicit error rather than guessing. A wrong series silently accepted would be worse than no result; the design treats it that way.

Processing covers longitudinal registration of the imaging pair, intra-session alignment of pre- and post-contrast series, and brain extraction using established, widely used tools.

03 — Provenance

Registration you can replay

Every run records its provenance: the registration method, the identity of the registration policy, hashes of the actual inputs, the transform, and the execution environment — bound to the exported artefacts, so a result can always be traced to exactly what produced it.

On the path where the claim can be made, registration is deterministic and replayable: the same input reproduces the same result, and any change to a registration parameter changes the recorded policy identity. Where determinism is bounded rather than exact, it is characterised as such — we do not make a blanket "same input, same output" claim.

One principle sits above all of this: anatomical correspondence is never inferred from a method name. Successful registration and convergence are recorded as provenance — they are not treated as proof that the anatomy corresponds. Where correspondence is unsupported, that lane abstains.

04 — Candidates

Direction-aware presentation

Candidate regions of change carry their location, volume and direction of change. New or increasing change and resolving or decreasing change are handled as separate ranked lanes, because they mean different things clinically and deserve separate attention.

Candidates are ordered by review priority, presented to the reading radiologist, and each one is explicitly accepted or rejected. The software detects change at the level of image regions; it does not count lesions, match lesions between visits, or classify disease — and it makes no diagnostic decision.

05 — Quality control

Graded outcomes, explicit abstention

The quality-control logic assesses whether the longitudinal comparison meets its prerequisites, and grades the outcome: report, report with caution, or an explicit refusal to report the longitudinal comparison. Abstention is a first-class output — when the comparison cannot be trusted, the software says so and withholds the result, rather than producing a number of unknown reliability.

Stated precisely: this does not guarantee that every unreliable result is caught. It means the system's own prerequisites are checked explicitly, and longitudinal output that fails them is withheld — with the refusal itself reported to the reader.

06 — Traceability

The build fails when the paperwork lies

Lävi Delta is developed under a documented software lifecycle (IEC 62304, Class B) with an ISO 14971 risk file, a requirements specification, traceability from every requirement to its tests, a controlled-document set and a change log.

That traceability is machine-checked, not merely tabulated. Automated guards fail the build when a requirement cites a test that does not exist or cannot be collected, when a controlled record is malformed, or when a controlled document drifts out of agreement with the shipped code. These guards exist because our own audits once found traceability rows that did not hold — a lesson we chose to encode into the build rather than into a policy document.

A large, continuously run automated test suite backs this, executed across multiple runtime versions in continuous integration.

07 — Conformance

Verified end to end, against a real deployment

The full producer-to-reader chain has been executed against a real deployment, not simulated: a committed pipeline run, exported as a hash-bound case package, delivered into the image store, ingested by the reader workstation, and presented to the reader solely from the authoritative package manifest.

The negative path was verified too: cases the pipeline rejects are rejected by the chain, not silently passed through.

08 — Review

A browser-based reading environment

For the planned reader study, a dedicated browser-based reader workstation exists and is deployed — built on a pinned open-source medical imaging viewer with a standard image store behind a phase-aware proxy.

It implements role-based access control with two-factor authentication, the blinding gate between study arms, washout enforcement between phases, structured reader response capture, an audit trail, and a results export. All of it is implemented and tested today.

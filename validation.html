Clinical validation & evidence

What we will prove, and how.

We claim nothing about clinical performance yet, because the evidence for such a claim does not exist. This page describes the study that will produce it — and where the boundary between technical and clinical evidence runs.

Clinical validation programme in preparation · Status as of 2026-11-17

01 — The question

What a reader study answers

Does a radiologist read follow-up brain MRI better with Lävi Delta than without it? That is the question, and we intend to answer it with an independent reader study rather than a marketing sentence.

A second-reader tool makes no findings of its own. What it changes — if it changes anything — is how a radiologist reads. So "how accurate is the software?" is the wrong question to lead with.

The unit of evaluation is not the algorithm. It is the radiologist reading with it.

The design built for that question is the multi-reader, multi-case study, MRMC for short. Five radiologists read the same retrospective cases twice in one session: first without the software, confirming that reading, and then with it. Because every reader reads every case both ways, the study can attribute a change in reading performance to the software itself, rather than to one sharp-eyed reader or one easy case mix.

That is the standard of evidence we consider appropriate here, and this site holds itself to it: until the study is run and independently analysed, no clinical performance claim appears on any page.

03 — The planned study

A retrospective multi-reader, multi-case study

The study in preparation uses retrospective brain MRI from 100 patients across three clinical strata: 40 with multiple sclerosis, 30 with a primary brain tumour and 30 with brain metastases. Each of the five participating radiologists reads every case twice in one session: first without Lävi Delta, confirming that reading, and then with Lävi Delta.

The reference standard and the statistical analysis are planned to be independently defined and reviewed. The endpoints being prepared cover localisation performance, false-positive burden, reading time and reader confidence, alongside standalone technical performance.

We publish no sample-size justification, no power figures, no target effect sizes and no dates. Those belong in the study protocol, not on a website.

04 — The reader environment

The study infrastructure already exists

Reader studies fail on logistics about as often as on science, so we built the reading environment first. A dedicated reading application that runs in a web browser exists and is installed — not a slide deck, a running system.

What is implemented and tested in it: while a radiologist reads without the software, its results are locked away and cannot be opened, even on purpose; every answer the reader gives is recorded in the same structured form; every action is logged; and the results can be exported for the independent analysts.

The analysis and this reading environment have been run together from start to finish; what that test covered.

05 — The boundary

What exists, and what is not yet established

Built and tested

  • Software that runs inside the hospital network: scans in from PACS, analysis without anyone pressing a button, results back to PACS in the standard format.
  • A quality check on every comparison, which withholds the result when two scans cannot fairly be compared.
  • A record of how every result was produced, attached to the result itself.
  • A dedicated reading environment for the planned study, which keeps the software's results hidden during unaided reading, controls who can see what, and logs every action.
  • Every requirement linked to the tests that check it, with the link checked automatically each time the software is built, and a large set of automated tests run continuously.
  • The whole path from analysis to reading screen, tested together on a complete installation set up the way it would be in a hospital.

Not yet established

  • Clinical performance. Whether Lävi Delta changes reader performance is exactly what the planned study exists to measure, and we will not claim the answer before the study does.
  • Comparison across scanners and vendors in patients. It is a design goal and an explicit requirement of the validation programme. In our internal technical tests on healthy volunteers, results across scanners from different vendors have been promising; what was tested. Confirmation in patients is still ahead, and different protocols have not yet been tested.
  • The two lanes, FLAIR and contrast-enhanced T1. Both are implemented and both are part of the planned reader study. Neither has been clinically evaluated yet, and no performance claim is made for either.
  • Capabilities still under technical evaluation, which are not part of any claim.
  • Regulatory conformity: the conformity-assessment stage lies ahead, as the pathway below shows.

An empty candidate list is not evidence that nothing changed. The radiologist reads every study independently; nothing about Lävi Delta reduces that responsibility, and nothing about it is meant to.

06 — Regulatory pathway

Stages, not dates

Lävi Delta is being developed as a medical device under the EU Medical Device Regulation. The pathway below has stages and no dates — on purpose. A published timeline is a promise about other people's decisions, and we do not make promises we cannot keep ourselves.

  1. Product and evidence developmentcurrent stage
  2. Technical verificationcurrent stage
  3. Independent clinical validationin preparation
  4. Quality and technical documentationahead
  5. Conformity assessmentahead
  6. Market deploymentahead

Lävi Delta is under development. It is not CE-marked, is not available for sale, and must not be used for clinical decision-making. Information on this site describes a product in development and its planned evaluation, not the performance of a marketed medical device.
